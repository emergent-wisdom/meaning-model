# Retirement without consistency

## Problem

How do we build a retirement system for people who don't know how much they will earn next month, where 'consistency' is impossible?

The problem of the Ontology of the Alien experiments.

## World regimes

- [regime.answered-decay] Answered decay: left alone, a thing's state does not stay; it relaxes, lapses, unravels or drifts, and it lasts only while something outside it answers that tendency: 0 direct, 3 in subtree
  operator: Every ordered thing is off the rest point of some spontaneous tendency: relaxation toward a ground, lapse toward the typical, unravelling, or an inward spiral. It cannot answer the tendency by its own form; it lasts only while something outside it does, and unanswered it degrades irreversibly. What must be supplied is persistence, not change: the unattended changes by itself, and effort or feed goes into staying. Worlds under this concept differ in what answers and what answering costs the answerer: a distinct holder that takes the displaced pressure into itself and must pass or shed it, failing one way, by relaxation, when holding lapses; or an ambient feed that others induce and nothing bears, failing two ways, by starving or tearing. Scarcity follows the answer: holding capacity where the answer is borne, a livable band of feed where it is not.
  partition regime.answered-decay.by-answerer, lens: what answers the tendency: borne (a distinct holder takes the displaced pressure and must pass or shed it; failure is one-way relaxation) versus unborne (an ambient feed that others induce; nothing accrues in suppliers; failure is two-sided, starving or tearing)

      operator: A state above its ground carries a one-way pressure to relax toward that ground. Holding does not dissolve the unbalanced pressure: it moves it into the holder, which must pass it to a sink. Unanswered excess relaxes irreversibly.

      operator: Every thing is a standing turning of one medium that can only flow; it keeps no material, only its pattern. Each carries a signed, conserved turn. - [regime.paid-change] Paid change: unattended state is kept exactly; every change is an agent's conserved, signed expenditure, lodged in what it changed and refundable only by its maker until overwritten: 1 direct, 1 in subtree
  operator: Things have no natures or tendencies, only conditions, and an unattended condition is kept exactly: nothing falls, coasts, cools, decays or is forgotten; there is no ground, up or down. Non-agents receive and keep change but never pass it on, so there are no mechanisms, chains or cascades. Spent currency is not consumed: it lodges in the change, signed by its maker, who alone can undo it for a full refund, and only while nothing has changed that thing since, so later work locks earlier work in stack order. No agent can take another's currency or alter another; it can only give currency, which cannot be refused. A witnessed transfer between agents creates an exact, permanent, bilaterally netted obligation that seizes the debtor's later agent-directed outflows, oldest creditor first, at any range. Witnessed renunciation fixes a change forever and deletes its currency from the world, so total currency only shrinks. What an agent is owed, plus what it has renounced, sets how fast it can spend.

### The Buttressed World, Where Only Children Stand Alone (world.buttress)

Seed: buttress (bank). Builder isolation: fresh_context. Target-blind task: yes.

Principle: In this world, weight never presses straight down. Every bit of matter presses on whatever lies beneath it downward and outward at the same time, the way the foot of an arch presses against its pier. Deep inside a mass, these outward pushes meet one another and cancel. At any surface steeper than its material can bear, though, the pushes behind it point into open air. Only the thin press of that air meets them, so the thing spreads. Its foot creeps outward and its top sinks until its sides are gentle enough that every push lands on matter again. That slope, the repose, is the steepest shape anything can keep on its own. So nothing holds itself up. A wall, a tower, a tree or a standing body exists only because something presses back on it where it would spread. That something is held in turn, down an unbroken chain of contact that ends in the Ground: the flat, endless floor of the world, already at repose and so the one thing that leans on nothing. The only strength there is, is the strength of a buttress. Solidity is a relation, not a property. A wall is solid because of what leans against it, and every steep thing is a claim on its neighbors. Left alone, the world relaxes into gentle mounds. Mountains are broad domes, and any steep face in a landscape means something is holding it. Tall cliffs survive only as hollow coves whose curved faces arch sideways into their flanks, and caves are always round. Forests are shaped like hills. The tallest trees stand in the middle, leaning crown to crown on flared plank roots, and the lowest, stoutest ones stand at the rim, so a forest cut at its edge dies inward. Large land animals are either low and dome-backed or live in packed herds, and predators hunt by pushing one animal out of the press. The tallest living things grow under deep water, which presses back harder than air. 2. The rules

- R1: Steepness pushes. Any face steeper than its material's repose pushes outward. The push at each point grows with the weight above it, so the total push on a steep face grows with the square of its height: double a wall's height and it needs four times the holding. Repose is a material's defining number. Loose earth reposes at one up for two across, fitted angular stone at one for one, and water and air at none. The surroundings press back on every face. Air presses lightly and evenly, enough to answer any face down to a depth of about a grown person's hip height below its top (the Hold), and no deeper. Water presses harder, and harder with depth, and buried things are answered completely. So anything shorter than the Hold may take any shape, while a taller steep face needs a buttress everywhere more than a Hold below its top.
- R2: Only a push answers a push. Nothing can pull. Matter has no cohesion of its own and carries only pushes, plus the friction those pushes create. A buttress must stand on the side a thrust points toward, touching what it holds. The air's press gives small things a faint hold against crumbling, enough for a thick cup to keep its water, but that hold carries no load. So there are no ropes, beams, lintels, cantilevers, hinges, levers, nets, sails or bowstrings. Every span is an arch and every lift is a push from beneath. Anything that must hold a push from inside, such as a tank, pipe or barrel, can do so only by being pressed harder from outside, which in practice means being buried.
- R3: Thrust is passed, never lost. An answered thrust is not destroyed; it is handed on. Where two faces press on each other equally, as two people hip to hip or two houses sharing a wall do, the pushes cancel and nothing passes. A packed crowd, granary or town therefore needs holding only at its outside. Where the pushes are unequal (a tall thing against a short one, a leaning thing, the foot of an arch), the difference travels onward through unbroken contact and adds up link by link until it reaches the Ground. The last buttress of a row of five leaning houses carries the lean of all five plus its own. Remove any link and everything upstream of it is unanswered in the same instant. Long chains also bow sideways and fail unless something braces them along their length.
- R4: Weight grounds thrust. The Ground takes any thrust, but only if it arrives no flatter than the Ground's own repose allows. On earth, which reposes at one up for two across, the weight pressing down through a foot must be at least twice the sideways thrust that foot passes, or the foot skids. On bare bedrock, called a holdfast, equal weight is enough. Weight is what turns a sideways push downward, so loading a buttress from above strengthens it, and an unladen buttress slides however well it is placed. Every body lives with both sides of this. The heavier it is, the more thrust it can ground while it stays low, and (by R1) the harder it pushes when it stands tall.
- R5: Spreading is permanent; raising needs centering. Unanswered thrust spreads a thing until it is back at repose. A small excess does this slowly (creep, over minutes to years), and a large one does it all at once (a slump). Nothing slumped rises again by itself: raised matter can be built but never restored. Anything raised above the Hold must be built inside temporary support, called centering, that carries every thrust until the permanent buttresses are in place. The dangerous moment is always the striking of the centering. Living things are the one partial exception. They grow by laying new tissue against old, so they are their own centering, and they can slowly mend a small creep if they are braced in time. When they die, they slump like anything else.

### The Unbroken Watch: Where Only the Watched Stay Themselves (world.vigil)

Seed: vigil (bank). Builder isolation: fresh_context. Target-blind task: yes.

Principle: Physics in this world has two halves. Process works much as ours does: things fall, burn, flow, grow and react. Persistence works differently, because nothing holds on to its own particulars. The quantity at stake is thisness. It means the lasting particulars (marks, grain, shape, wording, memory) that make this stone this stone rather than just a stone. Position, posture and motion are not thisness. Left alone, thisness drains away, and every particular thing slides toward the most ordinary member of its kind. Only one force runs against the drain, and that is vigil: a waking mind's attention, kept on a thing without a break. Vigil cannot create anything, and it cannot restore anything. It can only keep. So the detail of the world is a record of the attention it has received. Where minds have watched long and closely, the world is dense with particulars. Where no mind has watched, it is typical. The near face of the moon is watched every night by every eye on the planet, and it still carries every mark it has taken since the first eyes looked up. Its far side is smooth grey. Animals keep too. Herds post sentinels. Birds and dolphins sleep with one half of the brain at a time, so they keep themselves around the clock, which makes them the most individual creatures in the wild. Faces, texts, cities and species are all built up from vigil. Only the plainest stuff of the world lasts without vigil, present or past, and minds are the only keepers. 2. The laws

- R1: The Keeping. Only vigil holds thisness. Vigil is attention from a waking mind, aimed at a thing, without a break. - Vigil resists every loss of thisness, whatever the cause: lapse, wear, rot, fire, blade, healing, dying. - It holds harder the more thisness is at stake. Planing a rough plank meets almost no resistance. Breaking an old carved door while someone watches it meets a great deal. - It does not resist motion. It also does not resist any change that adds more particulars than it takes away. So under watch, detail tends only to build up. - A gap shorter than the grace (about one slow breath, five seconds) does not count, so blinks and glances are safe. A doze, a distraction or a walk to the door breaks the vigil. - A vigil passes from one keeper to the next only if the new keeper takes hold within the grace. In practice, keepers overlap.
- R2: The Lapse. Whatever is not kept loses thisness. - It goes typical first. Then it steps down a ladder of kinds toward its materials. It stops only at the Ground: stone, water, air and grey. Grey is the fine silt that all made and once-living things end as. - An unkept book goes from this book, to a typical book of its sort, to cliché, to words without sense, to marks, to blank paper, to pulp, to grey. - Lapse runs one way only. A renewed vigil stops it where it is and restores nothing. - It runs fastest on what is intricate and new. Detail that formed an hour ago fades within hours if no one sees it. A thing that is already typical has almost nothing left to lose. - Why the Ground needs no keeping at all is the oldest open question in this world's physics.
- R3: The Grain. A vigil keeps a thing only down to the finest detail its keeper can make out. - Sight keeps surfaces at a distance, but only in light. Touch keeps shape and texture within reach. Hearing and smell keep only the fact that the thing is still there and still its kind. - Grain gets coarser with distance and with breadth. Someone watching a whole square keeps its buildings but not their carvings. - Grain gets deeper with understanding. A keeper who knows a clock's gears keeps the gears; one who sees only a box keeps a box. A reader makes out letters where someone who cannot read sees only texture. - When several people watch one thing, everything any of them can make out is kept.
- R4: Tenure. An unbroken vigil cures what it keeps, and that stored keeping is called tenure. - Tenure grows with the square of the unbroken span, so a vigil twice as long cures four times as deep. A break keeps the tenure already gained, but the count starts again from zero. - Tenure is spent as a shield. An unwatched thing draws it down and lapses slowly until it runs out, then at full speed. - Forty unbroken days of watching let a newborn sleep a night alone with almost no loss. Forty separate days of daytime watching give it less than a hundredth as much. - Kinds have tenure too: the sum of every vigil ever kept over any of their members. Ancient kinds such as human, oak, bread and wheel are nearly unshakable. A new kind, such as the first machine of its design, slides into the nearest older kind it resembles. A kind whose members no one keeps drains away over generations.
- R5: The Burden. Lapse is never cancelled, only moved. - Whatever thisness a vigil saves, the keeper carries as weariness. It is light for a long-cured wall, heavy for a newborn and crushing for a burning house. - Everyone keeping the same detail shares the load. But each keeper can carry only so much, and any excess drains the keeper's own thisness instead. - Weariness is shed only in sleep, where it burns off as dreams. That is why dreams are full of faces and places going typical. - A sleeper keeps nothing, not even themself. A burdened sleeper whom no one keeps discharges part of the day's weight into their own thisness. A keeper who refuses to sleep starts to lapse while awake.

### The Turning Deep: Where To Be Is To Turn (world.whirlpool)

Seed: whirlpool (bank). Builder isolation: fresh_context. Target-blind task: yes.

Principle: The world has one substance, the Deep. It has no grain, no parts and no weight of its own. All it can do is flow. Everything else is a whirlpool in the Deep. Nothing is made of anything. The Deep passes through every whirl all the time: it is drawn in at the rim and let go through the eye. So no thing keeps its material for even an instant. Only the turning lasts, and a thing is simply its turning. Four properties describe any thing completely: - hand: deasil (clockwise seen from above) or widdershins (counterclockwise). - turn: how strongly it rotates. This does the work that mass and charge do in other worlds. - eye: a tight eye makes a hard, dense thing. A wide eye makes a soft, diffuse one. - draw: how hard it drinks the Deep. This is its weight. Some examples: - A stone is a fine checkerboard of tight whirls of both hands. It is still inside and quiet from outside. - Spume is made of loose, wide-eyed whirls that last only moments. - A person is a braid of thousands of small whirls turning in step around one heart-eye. Each person has a net hand. Whirls act on each other in only one way. Each one stirs the whole Deep around it, instantly and to any distance, and the stir fades as distance grows. There is no pushing, no contact force and no light. Every motion, every sense and every exchange is one whirl being carried or fed by the stir of others. The world is itself a whirl, the largest one known, called the Maelstrom: - It turns deasil around a calm central Eye, with a Throat beneath the Eye. - Fresh Deep pours in across the far Rim. Spent Deep leaves down the Throat and is never felt again. - People live on the Floor, a broad shelf of settled whirls that slopes gently toward the Eye. The world, like everything in it, is a standing shape with no permanent substance. 2. THE LAWS

- R1: The Draw. - Every whirl drinks the Deep. This pulls the Deep, and everything in it, into an inward spiral toward the whirl's eye. - The pull grows with the draw. Between two people it is too faint to notice, except as the dust that circles a sleeper. - Nothing falls straight. A drawn thing circles whatever draws it, and each circuit is tighter and faster than the last. At half the distance it moves twice as fast. - So no orbit is stable, and everything ends at an eye. - At the scale of a hand, the Draw is weight. A dropped cup spirals down and lands a little deasil of the spot below where it was let go. - At the scale of the world, the Draw is drift. ### The Kept World: Where Every Change Is Someone's Gift (world.potlatch)

Seed: potlatch (bank). Builder isolation: fresh_context. Target-blind task: yes.
Commissioned by com.world-4.

Principle: In the Kept World there is only one cause: a gift. A change happens only when a person gives some of their store, which is the world's single currency of change. The receiver then takes exactly the change that store paid for. Things have no natures or tendencies, only conditions. So the stillness relation is not an extra law. It is simply what happens when there is no other cause: if no one is giving to a thing, nothing can make it different, and it stays exactly as it is. Potlatch shapes everything else. Giving is the only way to act on the world. Being given to binds you. Your power is measured by what you have given away and not yet had back. People reason about this as calmly as we reason about a locked drawer: whatever you left is still there, exactly as you left it. Every "why did this change?" has the answer "someone paid for it", and there is always a name to find. Children learn this with their first toy. A dropped cup hangs in the air, and a toy that moved always had a mover. 2. The rules

- R1: Keeping. Whatever no one is giving to stays exactly as it is in every way: place, shape, heat, colour, freshness, tension, memory, mood and debt. - Nothing falls, flows, cools, spreads, rots, rusts, ages, heals, fades or is forgotten on its own. - A kettle heated a century ago is still exactly as hot. A drawn bow stays drawn forever and never looses its arrow. Spoken words hang in the air where they were said. - A gift of motion is a gift of a journey. The giver decides where the thing goes; it goes there and stops. Nothing coasts past what was paid for, and there is no up or down. - For anything untouched, its state now is its state at its last gift.
- R2: The Gift. - Only people give. Everything else (stone, water, air, grain, beasts) is a thing. A thing can receive a change and keep it, but it never gives, so it can never pass a change on. - A thrown stone that hits a window stops against it. The window breaks only if someone pays to break it. No lever, spring, wheel, fire or current does any work. Every effect is paid for directly, by the giver's own touch. - A change costs its giver store equal to its size. One measure carries a grown person's weight a hundred paces, brings a kettle from cold to boiling-hot, or leaves about ten words hanging in the air. - A waking hour of looking, listening and thinking costs about one measure. Seeing needs no light, because it is something the seer does. - Store is never used up. It sits in the change it bought, and every change carries its maker's name, readable by anyone who looks closely. - Only the maker can undo a change, and only if nobody has changed that thing since. Undoing gives the maker back the full store, so a round trip or a corrected mistake costs them nothing. - No one can take store from a person. It leaves a person only when they give it. - The only change one person can make to another is to give them store. Your body, place and mind change only when you spend on them, so no one can move, wound or kill anyone else.
- R3: Debt. - When a person receives store from another person, with at least one third person watching as witness, the receiver owes the giver exactly that much. - A gift cannot be refused. A gift given with no witness moves the store but binds no one. - Debts never fade (R1). Only repayment, which needs no witness, or the creditor's forgiveness ends a debt. - While you owe anyone, every measure you give to any person, witnessed or not, goes first to your creditors, oldest debt first, until you are clear. It reaches them wherever they are; debt is the one thing that travels without touch. Only what is left over reaches the person you meant. - Spending on things or on yourself is never redirected this way. - Between two people there is only one balance. If you return more than you owe, your debt is cleared and the other person now owes you the extra.
- R4: Standing. - Your standing is what others currently owe you, plus everything you have broken (R5). - Standing sets your pace, the fastest you can spend store. Everyone has 20 measures an hour, plus 1 more an hour for each measure of standing. - Store is how much you can do; standing is how fast. Receiving makes you rich but bound. Giving makes you fast but poor. Being repaid slows you down.
- R5: Breaking. - In front of witnesses, you may break a change you made, which means giving it up forever. - No one can ever undo it, though others may build over it. The store in it leaves the world for good, and your standing rises by that amount permanently, owed by no one. - Nothing adds store to the world, and breaking is the only thing that removes it. So the world's total store is fixed, except that every breaking makes it smaller.

### Mantle Grid: a Load-Bearing Pension (mech.mantle-grid, from world.buttress, condition F)

Operator: Additions of any size and timing count only by the amount present and are placed where they bear part of a slowly changing load that others stand on; the dependents pay the bearers in proportion to the load each part is measured to carry. The same dependence locks each part in place: a claim on it can change hands, but the part leaves only when a replacement takes its load.

Strangest element: There is no pot: savings exist only as load-bearing parts of what others stand on, so the dependence that pays the rent is the same dependence that locks the stone, and old age means joining the bearing layer rather than drawing down a store.. Kept as: Contributions are issued only as registered slices of in-service capacity counted in the reliability margin. No cash balance exists: a keyed unit is pledged or passed by deed, its hardware leaves service only when replaced, and retirees join the bearing layer, even hosting their units at home as a mantle battery.

Core mechanism: Any-size contributions buy slices of in-service firm grid capacity. Loads above a free base pay a convex standing due as a first lien, split monthly by each slice's metered load carried.

How it works: Payers route a tenth of each fee to units; savers add any amount, any time. Units inside the reserve margin are keyed: they earn the due, can be pledged by deed, and leave service only when replaced. Surplus units are loose: stored-energy credits spendable on your own bill. New large loads energize only after contracting loose units, which then key and earn. Peak-night flexibility is paid in units. Every elder gets common units at constrained nodes; retirees can host theirs as a home battery.

Why it fails: Big loads can self-supply, capping the due; regulators can bend its level; batteries age and cheaper tech can strand units; local units tie old age to the local economy unless pooled across grids; and the grid alone is too small to absorb every worker's savings.

### Long Round: relief-signed continuity (mech.long-round, from world.vigil, condition F)

Operator: Value that grows with the square of the unbroken length of a maintained state, and restarts its count at any gap, is supplied to each maintained party by a rotating pool of intermittent maintainers who are released and credited only when a successor confirms takeover. Because each maintainer contributes whichever resource they have spare at the moment, continuity becomes a property of the maintained party, produced by the pool's overlap rather than by any one member's regularity.

Strangest element: Continuity belongs to the kept and is made by others: the account is a chain of handovers, each credit minted by the successor, and its value grows with the square of its unbroken length, so the least regular member can own the longest chain.. Kept as: Every credit is minted by the next holder's signature at relief; the floor and the squared surplus share are keyed to the member's unbroken chain, not to payments; a lean month cannot break it, only exit can; moving circles is itself an overlapping handover.

Core mechanism: Each member always has a named holder; a holder is credited only when the successor signs 'I have it', adding a link to the member's chain; surplus is shared by squared unbroken span.

How it works: Circles of ~12 households, mixed by trade and lean season, rota a named holder for each member: monthly check-ins while working, nightly presence when frail, a vigil at death. A holder is released only on relief; a no-show extends the shift at double credit. Dues: money into one locked lifelong pot when busy, weighted care hours when idle. A bridge fund pays lapse-critical bills. Handover tokens sit in a replicated open ledger. Kept years earn the floor; squared spans share surplus.

Why it fails: Money still follows pay, so low earners get small pots. Hour credits need nearby demand, trust and vetting, and may be taxed as wages. Squared spans favour founders and make leaving costly. Grant-fed schemes often fade; frail elders stay heavy here. Close circles can turn nosy or coercive.

Curator: family admit_new (fam.relay-continuity); claimed outcome admit_new (o.kept-and-known)

### Delay-Line Share Pension (mech.delay-line, from world.whirlpool, condition F)

Operator: A fixed fraction is cut from each irregular inflow the instant it occurs and irrevocably released, in equal parcels, at many staggered positions upstream of a common collector on a one-way drift that needs no tending and that no party can halt or reverse, so each parcel returns after a delay set by its position. The collector's intake is thereby an average over many past inflows, and it releases a fixed fraction of its stock each period, divided by credit measured against each period's average cut.

Strangest element: Keeping is surrendered to the very drift that ruins keeping: each share is placed upstream at many distances, held by no one in transit, and the unstoppable inward creep delivers it, averaged, to whoever stands downstream.. Kept as: The candidate keeps no balance and has no manager. Each cut becomes frozen strips that only the calendar can move and that can land only in the common catch, in the years their payer would be old. Placement is the only decision ever made, averaging is done by the scatter rather than by judgment, and strips of the dead land for the living.

Core mechanism: A fixed share of each payment is cut at the rail and turned into frozen consumption-share strips maturing over the payer's old age into a common pool that pays a fixed fraction by points.

How it works: Payment rails split ~10% off every gain; a month with no gain owes nothing. Points = your cut / that month's average cut, split 50/50 in couples. Each cut buys sovereign strips paying shares of per-capita consumption, one per year of the payer's likely old age (survival-weighted). Strips sit frozen: no sale, loan or early cash-in. Matured strips land in one pool paying 1/24 of itself monthly by points; strips of the dead feed the living. A reserve slice pays a per-head floor in lean years.

Why it fails: No law of nature guarantees arrival: strips are only as good as the issuing state and its statistics office (Argentina bent its CPI). Cash gains dodge the rail; a cut with no emergency access may push workers into cash; real yields are low; a legislature can still unfreeze the registry.

Curator: family admit_new (fam.temporal-scatter); claimed outcome admit_instance (o.decoupled-income)

### Low-Water Frost Pension (mech.frost-line, explorer proposal, condition E)

Operator: Commitment is made by hindsight, not pledge: each month a fixed fraction of the balance the saver's everyday account has not dipped below over the trailing year (net of earlier frost) is frozen irreversibly into retirement claims. A lean spell pauses the freeze by lowering that low-water mark, so the lock only ever takes money that a full year of the saver's own need has already left untouched.

Strangest element: Nobody ever chooses a contribution: the saver's own lowest moment of the past year sets it, so hardship, not intention, decides how much becomes pension.. Kept as: There is no schedule, target, opt-in amount or fast lane; even a deliberate saver's money must sit a year like any other. The only way to save is stillness, the only way to stop is need, and no forecast ever replaces the frost line.

Core mechanism: Each month a fixed fraction of the balance the everyday account has not dipped below over the trailing year freezes irreversibly into inflation-indexed deferred income units.

How it works: Every inflow lands in one ordinary spending account; nothing is ever pledged. A meter marks the frost line: the part of today's balance untouched for a full year (the trailing-year low, net of earlier frost). Each month a sixth of the money under the line freezes irreversibly into inflation-indexed deferred income units paid from retirement, and a capped public match tops up each slice. A lean spell drags the line down and freezing pauses; unfrozen money stays spendable.

Why it fails: Lives with no idle money never frost, so chronic scarcity still needs a floor. A year worse than the last meets a buffer sized to a milder past, and money kept for a rare big cost may freeze first. The wary can dodge it by moving money out; cash-only lives show no balance.

### Tide Ledger of Owed Hours (mech.tide-ledger, from world.potlatch, condition H)

Operator: Transfers above a holder's reserve are split between a locked holding and interest-free claims on others; every claim is settled only out of the receiver's own later excess, oldest claim first, and a holder's priority for scarce shared capacity rises with what others still owe it, so a claim that is never settled gives the most lasting priority.

Strangest element: The best debtor is one who never repays: standing is what you have given and not had back, so an elder with nothing left who only spends on themself is a permanent source of standing for whoever carries them.. Kept as: Rank is claims outstanding plus support never returned, and it falls as claims are repaid. Support for a penniless elder is paid as housing and care only the elder can use, so it cannot flow back; it is the only rank that never falls, so patrons compete to carry such elders and the co-op never rations the floor. Bequests repay the patron first, with signed consent.

Core mechanism: Surplus above your buffer splits: half sealed in hours, half first to your oldest creditor, the rest lent interest-free to members in lean months. What others owe you sets your rank.

How it works: Members are paid through a co-op ledger that fills a buffer first. Of any surplus, half is sealed in hour units (member and co-op both hold keys); the other half repays the member's oldest creditors first, and the rest is lent interest-free to members in lean months, who repay only from later surplus. In rushes, jobs go first to members owed the most. Retirees live on sealed hours and repayments. Patrons who carry penniless elders gain rank that never falls.

Why it fails: Members in one trade share lean months, so the pool can empty exactly when it is needed. Surplus-only repayment invites hiding income off the ledger. Rank-for-priority entrenches the already busy. Hour units must be backed by real assets, and patrons may carry only elders who look good.
