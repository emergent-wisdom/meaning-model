# Lifecycle registry

Status: accepted breadth-first lifecycle registry for the first construction.

Every consequential Thing receives one complete lifecycle before the model
opens it into periods or episodes. A lifecycle is one Event with a beginning,
an end, and a coarse account of what persists and changes. Historical lives are
kept at year resolution here; exact dates can be anchored later without
reopening the plot. Fictional lives are explicitly authored counterfactual
material. A later scene may deepen any life, and may revise it if the deeper
account exposes a genuine contradiction.

The cast also follows the institutional constraints of 1840s Britain. Human
capacity is not assumed to differ by sex; access to recognized occupations and
authority plainly did. Formal
engineering, workshop ownership, actuarial office, capital control, and most
paid mathematical-clerical routes were overwhelmingly male, so the people who
hold those posts here are mostly men. Lovelace is an exceptional woman whose
access is explained by class, private education, scientific relationships, and
unusual ability, while her household, legal, bodily, and reputational limits
remain active. Margaret Halden's accounting knowledge and household work are
consequential without being converted into an implausible public office. This
is a historical constraint on opportunity, not a claim about innate ability;
the model will not erase women's agency, and it will not assign a woman an
exceptional formal role merely for balance without explaining how she reached
it.

## Thirteen complete human lives

### 1. Charles Babbage — 1791--1871 — historical, counterfactual after August 1843

Born into a prosperous banking family, Babbage survives an illness-disrupted
education, becomes a mathematical reformer at Cambridge, marries Georgiana
Whitmore, helps form scientific institutions, and devotes much of his adult
life and fortune to mechanized calculation. Bereavements in the 1820s, the
rupture with Joseph Clement, and the state's withdrawal from the Difference
Engine leave him both materially experienced and increasingly protective of
architectural authority. He develops the Analytical Engine, finds in Ada
Lovelace an exceptional interpreter, and in this counterfactual accepts a
bounded undertaking with her and Edward Halden. He helps produce one real
technical proof, experiences its material success as long-delayed joy and
recognition, then overreads its meaning and contributes to the failure of a
calculation office. He continues designing without completing the general
Engine and dies after choosing to preserve the proof and the failure together.

Opened deeply: yes, as a principal.

### 2. Ada Lovelace — 1815--1852 — historical, counterfactual after August 1843

Born into an aristocratic but divided household, Lovelace receives an unusually
serious mathematical education for a woman of her time while living under
strong maternal, marital, social, and bodily constraints. Illness interrupts
her youth; marriage to William King and motherhood organize much of adult life.
Her meeting with Babbage and later work with De Morgan prepare the Menabrea
translation and Notes, through which she becomes an original interpreter of
operations and symbols rather than merely a translator. In the counterfactual,
her 1843 proposal becomes a bounded authority over examples, evidence, and
claims. She helps make the apparatus testable, shares the pleasure of its
successful trial without enlarging the signed claim, distinguishes that success
from service capacity, and extends her published account of symbolic operations
into a distinction between mechanical consequence and judgment about supplied
premises and purposes. She initially protects her unusual claim authority by
leaving it dependent upon her own signature. As illness narrows her presence,
she recognizes the one-carrier weakness in both that authority and Neale's checking
function. She dies after asking for funded checking succession that the
surviving founders postpone and without a deputy to exercise her own bounded
authority.

Opened deeply: yes, as a principal.

### 3. Edward Halden — 1803--1874 — fictional

Born to a London commercial clerk and a mother who keeps the household
accounts, Halden receives practical schooling and is apprenticed to an
instrument contractor's office. He becomes a quantity estimator and works
agent, marries Margaret Bell in 1826, has two children, and learns railway and
precision-contract work without acquiring a university education, a workshop,
or inherited capital. George Farrow, known through railway and instrument
supply and also connected to Babbage's old workshop network, brings him a paid
feasibility inquiry in 1843. He earns a founding role by making the
undertaking finite through the creative reversal from pricing a machine part to
pricing an answerable claim. The completed trial makes that normally invisible
work briefly tangible and joyful; he then allows public recognition and
household hopes to turn four deposits into commitments beyond known checking capacity. After
authorizing an unchecked delivery, he discloses the error and closes the
insolvent office. He returns to ordinary salaried estimating, partially repairs
his standing, supplies the final account to Babbage, and dies without another
grand venture.

Opened deeply: yes, as a principal.

### 4. William King-Noel — 1805--1893 — historical

Born into the landed King family, William inherits wealth and position, enters
public life, marries Ada Byron in 1835, and becomes Earl of Lovelace in 1838.
His long life is organized around estates, family, and the obligations of rank.
He supports some of Ada's intellectual activity and supplies household
resources and access, while their marriage also embodies the legal and social
limits within which her work occurs. In the counterfactual he neither commands
the Engine undertaking nor disappears conveniently: he permits bounded
expenditure of Ada's time and travel, requires later work during illness to move
largely by correspondence, survives her death, raises their children, and
continues estate and public responsibilities until 1893.

Opened deeply: only if an Ada scene requires household authority, presence, or
money.

### 5. Joseph Whitworth — 1803--1887 — historical, bounded counterfactual role

Born in Stockport, Whitworth trains in mechanics, works in leading engineering
shops including those of Henry Maudslay and Joseph Clement, establishes his own
Manchester business, and becomes a central figure in precision measurement,
standardized screw threads, machine tools, arms, and engineering education. His
industrial standing and fortune grow far beyond the period of this story. In
the counterfactual he performs one paid review of gauges, interfaces, and
tolerances for the reduced apparatus. He does not become its founder,
financier, chief manufacturer, or convenient guarantor. The undertaking is a
minor episode in his much larger historical life, which continues independently
to 1887.

Opened deeply: no; one review episode unless a works scene proves essential.

### 6. Joseph Clement — 1779--1844 — historical

Born in Westmorland, Clement develops from artisan training into one of
Britain's leading precision mechanics and toolmakers. He works for major
engineers, establishes a London workshop, and becomes the indispensable maker
for Babbage's Difference Engine. Their collaboration produces extraordinary
components but collapses amid disputed costs, control, drawings, and
government-supported obligations. By the time of the 1843 divergence, the
partnership is over and functions principally as a living warning inside
Babbage's decisions and the supplier network through which Halden is reached.
Clement dies in 1844 and takes no counterfactual office or late reconciliation.

Opened deeply: no; historical parent process and one remembered cause of the
new contract structure.

### 7. Griffith Davies — 1788--1855 — historical, bounded counterfactual role

Davies rises from modest Welsh origins through mathematical and actuarial work
to become an important consulting actuary associated with Guardian Assurance
and the professional development of life-contingency calculation. His adult
life is built around making financial promises answerable to mortality data and
methods of calculation. He is intellectually curious about the card-directed
mechanism and has worked through Lovelace's Notes, so his insistence on evidence
is restraint rather than disinterest. In the counterfactual he agrees to a paid
feasibility comparison and later a bounded customer job because the question,
inputs, and acceptance evidence are explicit. He does not finance the Engine or surrender
customer authority to its founders. In 1854 he identifies the returned table's
discrepancy and invokes the correction and cancellation clause. He dies in
1855, the same year the office closes, without becoming part of its succession.

Opened deeply: one customer life episode around test and return.

### 8. Henry Prevost Babbage — 1824--1918 — historical, bounded counterfactual role

Born into Charles Babbage's family during the Difference Engine years, Henry
grows up amid bereavement, drawings, mechanisms, and his father's unfinished
work. He follows a military career in the East India Company's service rather
than becoming the venture's conveniently available young engineer. In 1871 he
is present as family and practical custodian when his father's life ends and
inherits drawings, workshop material, and relics. In the counterfactual the
complete office packet passes with them. Later in life he continues real work
on portions of his father's machinery and advocates for the designs, but the
model does not infer that the failed office created a continuous computing
industry. He dies in 1918.

Opened deeply: only at the 1871 custody episode.

### 9. George Farrow — 1796--1860 — fictional

Born to a London metalworker, Farrow apprentices in turning and instrument
making, becomes a foreman, and establishes a small precision works in Lambeth
in 1831 with borrowed family capital. He marries, employs between twelve and
twenty workers as orders vary, and survives by refusing specifications whose
inspection conditions are undefined. He forwards Babbage's initial inquiry to
Halden rather than price an unbounded Engine himself; Halden brings him the
reduced apparatus only after Whitworth's bounded review. Farrow manufactures the artifact under
staged payments, bears the 1846 composition failure without accepting sole
blame for a jointly designed interface, and backs his foreman's honest finding
although doing so delays the works' payment. He completes the correction and performs
maintenance through 1855. The office closure costs him a customer but not his
works; he continues ordinary instrument contracts until illness leads him to
sell the business in 1858, and he dies in 1860.

Opened deeply: one manufacture episode; otherwise the organization carries the
process.

### 10. Margaret Halden — 1806--1881 — fictional

Born Margaret Bell in Southwark to a stationer's family, she learns household
and shop accounts, marries Edward Halden in 1826, and raises their two children
through irregular contracting income. She never receives a fictitious public
office in the Engine venture, but she is part of the real cost of Edward's
choices: the £100 investment, the late years of construction, the hope that a
successful office might make the family secure, and the loss at closure. She
supports the initial bounded commission because it is paid and defined, sees
the office expansion as both opportunity and danger, and refuses after 1854 to
let Edward describe disclosure as a sacrifice borne only by himself. She
returns to supplementary stationer's bookkeeping while he rebuilds ordinary
employment and outlives him by seven years.

Opened deeply: only if the story needs to make Halden's means and well-being
concrete rather than merely numerical.

### 11. Thomas Neale — 1819--1868 — fictional

Born to a schoolmaster in Clerkenwell, Neale becomes a mathematical clerk and
earns a reputation for patient independent checking rather than invention. He
works first in assurance copying and joins the Calculation Office for the 1851
trial and recurring service. The office's true verified capacity depends more
on his scarce hours than its founders admit. He begins teaching Wills but will
not misdescribe supervised work as an independent check. In April 1854 he is assigned to a
different promised packet when Guardian's job is released without his second
signature; he neither makes the transposition nor possesses authority to halt
all deliveries. During correction he gives Wills the active reading role and
puts his own name beneath their paired work rather than isolating the junior
clerk. Closure sends him back to assurance work, where the public
failure makes employment temporarily harder but his exact trace protects him
from blame. He continues as a clerk and checker until his death in 1868.

Opened deeply: at the capacity review and omitted-check episode.

### 12. Samuel Wills — 1828--1890 — fictional

Born to a printer's compositor in London, Wills receives elementary schooling,
learns careful copying, and enters clerical work because it offers steadier
prospects than the print shop. He joins the Calculation Office as a junior
preparer, converting customer sheets into operation and value cards. Under
overlapping deadlines in 1854 he transposes 3,742 as 3,472 and reads his own
copy back to himself after the independent checker is unavailable. The mistake
is his, but the unauthorized substitution of self-check for independent check
is Halden's institutional decision. Wills loses the position at closure, later
finds work in railway accounts, becomes unusually strict about paired copying,
supports a family, and dies in 1890 without being reduced for life to one error.

Opened deeply: at the returned-table episode and, if useful, one later glimpse
showing adaptation rather than punishment as closure.

### 13. Mary Somerville — 1780--1872 — historical

Born in Scotland, Somerville largely educates herself against the expectations
placed upon girls, develops into a mathematician and scientific writer of
international standing, and builds intellectual authority through synthesis,
translation, experiment, and unusually clear exposition. Her second marriage
supports rather than ends her work. She becomes an important model and contact
for Lovelace and helps connect her to the scientific world in which Babbage is
encountered, before moving to Italy for family health. During the
counterfactual she remains a correspondent rather than an invented manager of
the London undertaking. Her long productive life demonstrates the rule the
cast uses: brilliance is not the historical rarity; sustained access,
recognition, and formal power are. She dies in 1872.

Opened deeply: no, unless a Lovelace passage needs one letter that locates her
intellectual life beyond Babbage.

## Progressive-detail rule now in force

The registry is enough to begin. A life is opened only when a selected event
needs knowledge, motive, presence, relationship, or adaptation that the coarse
life does not answer. If the opening remains compatible, it becomes a child of
the life. If it exposes a local weakness, the local account is edited. If it
shows that the complete life could not plausibly produce the parent story, the
life or story is revised immediately. No new audit process is required to make
that authorial judgment.
