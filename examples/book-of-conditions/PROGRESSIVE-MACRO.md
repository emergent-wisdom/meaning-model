# Progressive macro account

Status: accepted macro understanding for the first construction.

Purpose: establish one coherent causal world before opening records. The five
accounts below are successive expansions of the same proposed history, not five
alternatives. Each expansion must preserve or explicitly revise the causal
claims above it. These paragraphs are understanding text; the later world model
must supply the Things, Events, Bindings, Cuts, descriptions, and evidence that
make the final account true.

## One paragraph

After the government ends support for the Difference Engine, Babbage nearly
repeats the same pattern with the Analytical Engine, but in August 1843 he keeps
negotiating with Lovelace instead of rejecting her proposed conditions. Their
agreement creates a route to a third, commercially trained founder who will
undertake only a paid feasibility inquiry and then organize a bounded machine
under staged finance, independent manufacture, and an acceptance test, while
Babbage remains free to develop later improvements separately. Cost and fit
failures force the undertaking to narrow and correct itself, but a limited
independently witnessed trial eventually succeeds and its result is published.
That success becomes the largest shock in the
story: Babbage reads it as proof of the architecture, the venture founder reads
the resulting inquiries as proof of a scalable service, and Lovelace reads it
only as proof of the frozen test. Babbage and the founder expand beyond recorded
checking capacity despite her caveat; after her death ends her active pressure
for a funded checking succession, a human preparation error passes an omitted
check, customers demand correction and repayment, and the office
closes. The general Engine is never completed, but Babbage eventually preserves
the successful proof together with the caveat, error, accounts, and closure, so
the world changes not through an Engine Age but through one complete record of
what a valid claim required and why a true demonstration did not make a viable
institution.

## Two paragraphs

After the government ends support for the Difference Engine, Babbage brings the
same conflict between changing design and finite obligation into the Analytical
Engine. In August 1843 he chooses a limited continuation of negotiations with
Lovelace. Her conditions do not themselves finance or manufacture anything;
they require a route by which another person can price, schedule, and be held
answerable for a bounded undertaking. Through the existing engineering network,
they reach a commercially trained works agent who accepts a paid feasibility
commission rather than the whole vision. A frozen production baseline, a
separate register for later inventions, a priced works review, staged outside
funding, and independent acceptance make a small machine possible. Material
failures and a credit contraction slow the work and cause rework, but the gates
prevent them from silently enlarging the promise. An independently witnessed
trial eventually establishes one narrow technical result, which is then
published.

The trial alters the organization more than it alters the machine. Public
interest gives Babbage recognition, gives the venture founder a chance to become
more than an estimator, and gives Lovelace evidence that the conditions work;
the same event therefore pushes their expectations in different directions.
The founder and Babbage accept more calculation work than the office can prepare
and check, while Lovelace records that technical proof is not capacity. Her
declining health makes succession foreseeable, but the undertaking postpones
funding and assigning her procedural role. After her death, overload turns that
omission into a customer error: the mechanism follows its inputs correctly, but
a manually prepared value escapes because the promised duplicate check is
skipped. Liability, refunds, and unpaid obligations exhaust the venture. The
founder closes the office and discloses the accounts; Babbage later deposits the
plans with the evidence that limits them. By 1900 no machine or office lineage
survives, but the complete documentary packet remains in custody.

## Three paragraphs

The counterfactual begins from two documented failures rather than a sudden
change of temperament. The break with Clement showed that an intricate machine
could consume money without creating a jointly governable product, and the 1842
government decision removed the expectation that public patronage would absorb
open-ended revision. Lovelace's 1843 proposal gives Babbage a choice between
refusal, unconditional personal control, and a temporary negotiation. He chooses
the temporary route. Because neither of them has the authority or practical
occupation needed to turn that choice into a contract, a bounded inquiry is
sent into the existing engineering network. A fictional but period-compatible
works estimator becomes the third principal only after he returns an adequate
feasibility account and accepts personal professional exposure to the result.

The new founder coordinates rather than replaces the network. Babbage owns the
architecture; Lovelace specifies operations, evidence, and limits; an
independent works owns manufacture and inspection; a customer owns its problem
and acceptance; capital custodians release money only at declared gates. The
first commitment is therefore not the Analytical Engine as imagined, but a
bounded mechanism whose present configuration is frozen while improvements are
recorded outside it. Inspection reveals that some individually successful work
will not compose, and the 1847 contraction threatens the remaining cash. The
undertaking adapts through delay, rework, and narrower claims instead of adding
unfunded scope. A sealed comparison with independent human results eventually
passes. This proves that the bounded configuration performed the declared
calculation; it does not prove speed, economy, generality, or repeatable service.

Prestige erodes the distinctions that hardship had enforced. Babbage treats the
trial as recognition of the general architecture. The founder treats dated
inquiries and deposits as a way to finance an office and secure professional
standing. Lovelace insists that demand must be compared with the labor needed to
prepare, verify, correct, and deliver each job. Babbage urges work beyond that
capacity and Halden authorizes it; both fail to create a funded successor to her
role before she dies. A later delivery contains a humanly introduced error that the machine
correctly propagates; the office had omitted the duplicate check it promised.
The customer invokes the agreed correction and cancellation terms. The
founder's final consequential choice is to expose his own approval in the
ledger, repay what the remaining cash permits, and close. Babbage's later growth
is equally bounded: he does not complete the Engine or redeem the failure, but
he allows the proof, warning, error, debt, and closure to remain one inseparable
record.

## Four paragraphs

From 1833 to 1842, failure teaches Babbage the wrong lesson as well as the right
one. He learns that contractors and governments can abandon a machine whose
design they do not understand, but also learns to protect himself by keeping
design authority fluid. His Analytical Engine work grows intellectually while
its path into matter grows weaker. Lovelace's Notes make the architecture
legible at precisely the moment the state refuses further expenditure. In
August 1843 her governance proposal threatens the strategy by which he has
protected both the idea and himself. He does not accept her scheme wholesale;
he accepts a time-limited negotiation whose first question is whether any
finite obligation can preserve the general architecture without pretending to
embody all of it.

That question produces the missing founder rather than the reverse. A bounded
cost inquiry reaches a London works estimator through the earlier engineering
network. The estimator refuses to promote or finance an unspecified Engine. He
earns a continuing role by separating the committed configuration from later
changes, mapping suppliers and dependencies, obtaining an independent priced
review, and writing stages at which investors or a customer may stop. His
authority covers cost, schedule, contracts, and acceptance consequences, not
architecture or workshop judgment. When manufacture later exposes an interface
that cannot meet the accepted assembly, Babbage proposes an improvement,
Lovelace requires the disagreement and rejected drawing to remain visible, and
the founder enforces rework under the frozen claim. A wider credit shock slows
the undertaking, but protected obligations and reduced pace keep it alive.

The witnessed test is designed before the final machine is ready: sealed cases,
independent calculation, declared exclusions, duplicate runs, and a stopping
rule. Its success in 1851 is therefore real and limited. Yet success changes the
three principals asymmetrically. Babbage receives the public recognition that
government refusal denied him. The founder sees a route from precarious fees to
an enduring professional institution. Lovelace sees that the evidential method
worked and that the institution is now in danger of claiming more than the
method established. A capacity review shows that customer interest exceeds
preparation and checking labor. Babbage and the founder nevertheless accept a
larger book of work: one wants the architecture acknowledged through use; the
other wants the office to survive and his organizing work to count as making.
Lovelace attaches a signed qualification and asks who will hold the checking
function as her health fails. They defer the answer.

Her death ends an active role but does not mechanically cause the later error.
It leaves a known responsibility without an adequate successor while deadlines,
cash needs, and prestige continue. In 1854 an input-preparation mistake enters a
customer table, the bounded mechanism computes from it correctly, and an
overloaded office skips the independent comparison that would have caught it.
The contract makes the consequences concrete: correction, cancellation,
refund, unpaid balances, and loss of reputation. The founder cannot save the
venture without hiding obligations or finding unsupported capital; he chooses
closure and records his own part in the expansion. Babbage initially narrows the
failure to human misuse, then later accepts that a computational system includes
the conditions around the mechanism. Before his death, the complete packet
moves into named custody. The 1900 world contains no general Engine, operating
office, or implied revolution—only documentary descendants of the test and
correction practice.

## Five paragraphs

The long process begins before the divergence. Between the rupture with Clement
and the government's 1842 withdrawal, Babbage's project loses both a working
manufacturing relation and an external payer. He adapts by investing still more
of the project in design, notation, and personal architectural control. That
adaptation helps produce the Analytical Engine, but it also makes every finite
version feel like an injury to the larger one. Lovelace's Notes reveal the
machine's general meaning while her August 1843 proposal asks for powers and
obligations that could survive enthusiasm. Babbage's first counterfactual act is
small: after receiving the proposal, he agrees to continue for a fixed interval
instead of ending the negotiation. Nothing has yet been built, financed, or
promised.

The negotiation exposes what the pair lack. Lovelace can formulate conditions
and Babbage can define mechanisms, but neither can credibly price suppliers,
control staged payments, or accept commercial responsibility for delivery. A
post-divergence inquiry through the old engineering network reaches a London
estimator and works agent whose pre-1843 life is authored fictional background
that changes no documentary event. He accepts only a paid feasibility task.
During 1843–44 he produces a configuration register, a supplier and dependency
map, cost and schedule ranges, inspection gates, payment stops, and a procedure
for separating changes to the committed build from successor work. An
independent works and a prospective customer must each agree to their own
bounded role; investors retain custody of cash. Only when scope, price, test,
and staged funding align does the estimator become the undertaking's commercial
founder and a third principal. His household income and professional reputation,
not unlimited private wealth, are placed at risk.

Manufacture from the mid-1840s to 1851 tests the agreement. Some parts meet
their drawings but fail when assembled, revealing that local success is not a
composed machine. Babbage's technically attractive correction would reopen
accepted interfaces; the founder initially refuses it, the works supplies
material evidence, and Lovelace records both the failure and the deferred
improvement. A credit contraction reduces available cash and stretches the
schedule. The undertaking responds by protecting already earned wages, slowing
work, and keeping the bounded configuration rather than promising a grander
machine. These decisions make a controlled trial possible. Under sealed inputs,
an independent human baseline, repeated runs, and declared exclusions, the
mechanism passes in 1851. The exact result is a bounded technical proof, not a
completed Analytical Engine and not proof of a viable calculation industry.

The proof is a positive shock that defeats the founders' earlier adaptation.
Dated inquiries, deposits, and public attention arrive before the office has
trained enough preparers and checkers. Babbage assigns the success to the
architecture and presses for visible work. The commercial founder assigns it to
the staged organization he built and begins treating demand as evidence that
capacity can be financed after commitment. Lovelace distinguishes both readings
from what the test actually warranted. A review shows a small verified service
capacity, but Babbage urges and Halden authorizes more obligations than it can
carry, for different wants and through mutually reinforcing confidence. Lovelace uses
her limited documentary authority to attach a capacity warning and to demand a
successor for checking and correction. Her illness is anticipated; the loss of
her active role is not prepared for. She dies in 1852 with the succession
question still open.

During 1853–55 the office attempts to adapt by compressing preparation and
checking around the enlarged contracts. A manually prepared input is wrong; the
machine performs its bounded operation correctly; the independent duplicate
check is skipped to meet delivery. The customer returns the table and invokes
the correction, liability, and cancellation clauses. Because receipts, wages,
refunds, and remaining obligations are represented in one ledger, prestige
cannot make the shortfall disappear. The founder's gates enabled the proof and
his later compromise helped create the failure. He discloses both facts, signs
the available refunds, and closes the office rather than sell a new promise.
Babbage first treats the error as external to the Engine, but over the remaining
years accepts a narrower conclusion: the general architecture remains
unrealized, while the one valid result is inseparable from the human and
institutional conditions that made and then broke it. In 1871 the complete
record passes to a named custodian. At the 1900 horizon the artifact and office
are gone; the retained lineage consists of the frozen claim, the rejected
changes, the capacity warning, the erroneous delivery, the correction, and the
accounts.

## Questions closed by the world opening

- George Farrow, known to Halden through instrument and railway supply and to
  Babbage through the former Clement network, forwards the paid inquiry. Farrow
  may later bid for manufacture only after the independent Manchester review.
- Edward Halden's complete fictional life supplies a period-compatible
  estimator and works agent without changing any documentary-prefix event.
- The manufactured object is the six-register bounded card apparatus; its
  prospective trial establishes one frozen recurrence calculation and no
  general Engine, service rate, or industrial consequence.
- The construction and office accounts close in nominal pounds. Babbage,
  subscribers, Guardian, and Halden fund distinct obligations through a trust;
  customer deposits later belong to a separate office ledger.
- Farrow's works manufactures, Whitworth's works reviews, Guardian supplies and
  accepts the test question, Davies holds the comparison, the trust holds cash,
  and Henry Babbage receives final custody.
- Before expansion, the office records forty independent checker-hours per job
  and eighty available checker-hours per quarter: two verified jobs, against
  four deposits Halden later accepts.
- Lovelace's request to train and fund a second checker makes the succession
  cost explicit before her death. Babbage supports deferral and Halden, who
  holds contracting authority, defers the expenditure over her objection.
- The later failure is a transposition from 3,742 to 3,472 followed by Halden's
  dated decision to deliver without the promised duplicate check. Guardian's
  refund, correction cost, and closure are reconciled in one office account.

These closures are now commitments of the accepted world. Finer work may
repair a local route, but it may not silently reopen the parent history.

## Backtracking rule

Opening detail is a test of the parent account, but not every uncertainty is a
reason to restart it.

1. **Remainder:** names, exact dates, prices, minor participants, scene texture,
   and unopened mechanisms may remain unresolved while the coarser causal route
   is still possible. They stay explicit and receive no invented precision.
2. **Local repair:** if a detail is implausible but another detail can replace it
   without changing why the parent process moved, repair only that branch. A
   trial moving by a year, a different contractor, or a different document
   custodian normally belongs here.
3. **Parent failure:** supersede and rewrite the macro account when a necessary
   transition has no plausible cause; an Event requires unavailable knowledge,
   authority, money, labor, place, or time; quantities cannot reconcile; a
   character exists only to supply whatever the plot needs; or the proposed
   children jointly describe a different parent outcome. Do not conceal such a
   failure in the remainder.

The practical test is counterfactual: if the questionable detail were removed,
could the same macro transition still occur through an already represented
route? If yes, leave it open or repair it locally. If no, the parent explanation
has failed.

## Two editing loops

The world may also be revised for dramatic reasons. A causally possible history
is still a weak candidate if choices have no cost, principals read every Event
the same way, shocks merely confirm what everyone already believed, or one
episode can be removed without changing any later process. That is a failure of
generative pressure, not a demand for spectacle.

Once the world has both causal coherence and pressure, later story projection
does not rewrite it merely to improve pacing. It selects the route through the
accepted Events that best exposes consequential choices, divergent readings,
changed relationships, and the difference between anticipation and hindsight.
The world loop asks whether an interesting history exists. The story loop asks
which small part of that history should be rendered.

This accepted world has four useful intersections: one technical success
is appraised differently by all three principals; the venture founder's
discipline can become overconfidence rather than infallibility; Lovelace's finite
lifetime makes deferred succession costly; and correct mechanism can coexist
with failed service. Those pressures justify further refinement without
prescribing the eventual scenes.
