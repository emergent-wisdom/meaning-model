# Fourth independent reader, version 4 (2026-09-23, night)

A fresh-context reader received only `export/integration-time.md` (v4, text sha 6d04e6dc…), with no model, graph, notes or earlier reports. Its assessment, verbatim:

**What it's about**

Graciela spends eleven months flying to a solar gravitational lens station at 672 AU, where her job is to automate away her mother's post. Candelaria has spent eleven years building a map of a black photosynthetic mat on TRAPPIST-1e, and it shows nothing moving. Graciela's single-window change detector finds a dozen swaths creeping east across the mat, a pattern the long integration had averaged flat and that may be cultivation. The ship's departure date is fixed. Each woman secretly arranges to stay so the other can go home, and in the end both stay, on food that runs out before the next ship.

**The science**

Almost every number checks. 672 AU is past the lens's 548 AU minimum focus. From there the Sun is 2.9 arcseconds wide and magnitude −12.6, a full Moon's light from a single point, so the knife-edged shadow is right. Light time is 93.1 hours and the trip averages 0.0116c. The planet's image is about 3 km across, and a one-metre aperture maps to 3.8 km on the ground, so the stated pixel is exactly one telescope wide. The Einstein ring sits at 1.1 solar radii, truly "a hair's breadth out." The swaths recur every 148 days, 27 times in eleven years, and twelve of them at 75 km spacing span the 900 km mat. The report's answer does land on day eighteen. The 670 person-days is two passengers for 335 days. The day-fourteen window at 06:05 falls one orbit after 03:40 on day eight, to within a couple of minutes. Tiger bush and fir waves are right, and the report hedges honestly.

Three things wobble. The claim that "an eight-kilometre swath is not there at all" overstates it. At forty kilometres a pixel the swath is diluted to a fifth, not erased, and binning raises signal-to-noise. Say instead that the cloud checks had a high threshold or compared orbit to orbit. Second, the ship's fusion torch is lit minutes after undocking, and they watch through the port that faces the Sun. That means its exhaust points at them, and it would not pass for a star. Third, the famine bet goes away once you do the next division: 2,130 person-days for two over four years is three-quarter rations, which these two would see instantly.

**The two women**

Their voices are distinct enough, and credible. Candelaria turns feeling into provisions ("Do you want tea? I have tea. Real leaves."; later, "The stores are fourteen hundred and sixty person-days"). Her syntax breaks only under pressure ("Two years is— he's waiting now, today."). Graciela is clipped ("I know what the stores are."), hides in explanation and says "copy." The mother's middle section is the best writing here, and "She did not, she noticed later, think about her daughter at all" is brave. The dialogue turns written in the comebacks ("So did I.") and in Graciela's big speech near the end. Both viewpoints also share one narrator's rhythm.

**Structure and ending**

Handing the discovery to the mother's viewpoint is the right call. The drama is her expertise ("They stop at the coast") overruling the machine's yellow fault box. The ending is mostly earned by its setups. Her habit of waking at three sets up her five o'clock visit to Kaya, "You timed it" returns as "She timed it," and "this time did not correct it" lands quietly. The contrived part is the double secret sacrifice: both women go to Kaya within the same hour, a Gift of the Magi that his laughter points out. The symmetry works once, but the closing pages repeat it three more times: "decided in the same room," the finished sentences, and a last play on "the answer."

The cost is thinner than it looks. "Food for nearly three years; the next ship in four" states it flatly. But the mother stays, as she wanted to, Tomás pays offstage in one good sentence, and rationing takes the danger out of the famine.

**Strongest and weakest**

Strongest:
- "Her mother was smaller than her handwriting." Eleven years of letters in seven words.
- "It isn't too noisy to disagree with one." The method and the reunion in one sentence.
- "She had integrated it away." Title, science and the mother's flaw come together.

Weakest:
- "and then he began, very quietly, to laugh." The laugh does the reader's recognizing for them.
- "self-organised banding by water disfavoured (no curvature, no forks, a single rate)." The report restates the paragraph we just read.
- "I got so good at waiting that I did it to Tomás." The theme as an epigram, and it slides from waiting to making someone else wait.

**Revisions**

1. Halve the report paragraph. Keep the tiger-bush and fir-wave reasoning in narration, cut the italics down to the interpretation line, and change "could not take back from here" to "could not rule out."
2. Put the cost in the body. Have Candelaria do the second division aloud (four years on three-quarter rations), so the ending costs hunger rather than faith in a committee, and cut Kaya's laugh.

**Verdict**

I'd buy it after a light rewrite: the science is unusually well worked and the discovery scene turns it into drama, but the ending needs one less echo and one real cost.
