# Fifth independent reader, version 5 (2026-09-23, afternoon)

A fresh-context reader received only `export/integration-time.md` (v5, text sha 479d3879…), with no model, graph, notes or earlier reports. Its assessment, verbatim:

**What it's about**

An engineer flies eleven months to a solar-gravitational-lens station at 672 AU. Her job is to automate it and bring home her mother, who has spent eleven years mapping a dark photosynthetic mat on TRAPPIST-1e. The daughter's new change detector finds moving swaths, apparently cut, across what her mother calls "the steadiest thing I know". The mother's long integrations had averaged them into stillness. Each woman then secretly arranges to stay so the other can go home. In the end both stay, on short rations, betting that Earth will believe them.

**The science**

The science is exceptionally careful, and nearly everything I checked holds:

- At 672 AU the Sun is 2.9 arcseconds across and magnitude −12.6, against a full Moon's −12.7. A source that small does cast a razor shadow, and its "edges as clean as a cut" quietly set up the final test of step versus slope.
- One-way light time is 93.2 hours, so a question and its answer take 7.8 days against TRAPPIST-1e's 6.1-day orbit. That mismatch drives the plot.
- At 40.7 light years the planet's image is about 3.1 km wide, and a one-metre aperture maps to 3.8 km on the ground.
- Eleven months to 672 AU averages 1.16% of the speed of light.
- The swaths recur every 148 days, 27 times in eleven years. The 03:40 and 06:05 windows are one orbit apart to within minutes. The 670 person-days are two passengers for eleven months.
- Black photosynthesis, fir waves and tiger bush are used accurately, and the story admits it cannot rule out a wind wave.

The flaws are small:

- **Gravity is never established.** Mugs sit on counters, there is a kettle, people sit, and Graciela "laid her kit out on the deck". Yet she "pulled herself on" through the tube and Kaya drinks from a bulb. A spinning station docked at the hub would reconcile these, but the story has to say so. Spin would also sweep the lentil of sunlight around the galley unless the axis pointed at the Sun.
- **The rations are slightly off.** The mother's "four years on three-quarter rations" is six weeks short: 2,130 person-days at 75% feed two people for 1,420 days.
- **The departure date is asserted, not explained.** At 672 AU there are no launch windows.
- **The key test is muddled.** "the stripes were in the same places on the ground" sits oddly beside edges that had all moved 3.1 km east. A uniform shift is also what a misaligned overlay looks like, so say the coastline held still.

**The two women**

They differ more in behaviour than in how they talk. Candelaria retreats into hospitality and numbers ("Do you want tea? I have tea. Real leaves."). Graciela retreats into procedure and says "copy" instead of "yes". The exchange about her double surname rings exactly true. But both speak in the narrator's clean, aphoristic register, and the rhythm of the prose doesn't change when the viewpoint passes to the mother. "That doesn't keep" could belong to either woman. The line that feels most written is "I got so good at waiting that I made Tomás do it", a thesis statement in quotation marks.

**Structure and ending**

There are three movements: daughter, mother, daughter. The middle is the best thing here, a real deduction shown in scene. The mother feels like "an old instrument caught out by a new one", and then catches the new instrument's misdiagnosis.

The ending is mostly earned. It has the shape of "The Gift of the Magi", and "She timed it" clicks because "You timed it" was planted early. "Send it now" saves it from being merely neat. The mother timed her own move that morning; now she watches her daughter make someone wait, as she once did, and breaks the habit for both of them.

The cost to Tomás is on the page. The hunger stays a number, and the mother gives in too fast. The last clause, about "how long the answer had taken", explains what Graciela finishing her mother's sentence has already shown.

**Strongest and weakest**

Strongest:
- "Her mother was smaller than her handwriting": eleven years of letters in seven words.
- "They stop at the coast": the discovery turns on one observation, and the right woman makes it.
- "Send it now, and it leaves while you could still change your mind": the timing motif becomes a moral act.

Weakest:
- "a torch kept to its propellant plan or did not get home": the one real hand-wave, crammed together with the crew and the speed into the paragraph after the best one.
- "tiger bush, the ecologists called it": the report stops for a literature survey. Cut it by half, but keep her taking out "consistent with" and putting it back.
- "Showing them it isn't one is work for two": an argument about staffing that settles eleven years too cheaply.

**Revisions**

1. Add a clause each to settle the gravity, fix the ration fraction, and say what held still when the maps were laid over each other.
2. Give the climax one more beat of resistance before "Both, then". Let Candelaria admit, or refuse to admit, that she wanted to stay all along, so her reversal visibly costs her something.

**Verdict**

Yes, with a light revision: rare hard SF in which the physics drives the emotion, held back only by continuity slips and a last act a notch too tidy.
