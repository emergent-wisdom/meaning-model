# Third independent reader, version 3 (2026-09-23, night)

A fresh-context reader received only `export/integration-time.md` (v3, text sha ee3e762a…), with no model, graph, notes or earlier reports. Its assessment, verbatim:

**What it's about**

Graciela Mardones travels eleven months to a solar-gravitational-lens station at 672 AU. She is there to install the software that will replace her mother, Candelaria, who has spent eleven years alone watching a black photosynthetic mat on TRAPPIST-1e. The daughter's single-window pipeline finds what the mother's decade of averaging erased: parallel swaths that appear to be cut in lockstep across the field. Each woman secretly arranges to stay so the other can go home. In the end they decide, in the same room for once, that both will stay.

**The science**

I checked the numbers and they are exceptionally clean. At 672 AU the Sun is 2.9 arcseconds across and magnitude −12.6, which is a full Moon. Light time is 93.1 hours, and a round trip is 7.8 days. The planet's image is 3.06 km wide, and a one-metre aperture maps to 3.83 km of ground. The Einstein ring sits 0.15 arcseconds outside the Sun's edge. Swaths 75 km apart moving 3.1 km per orbit recut each spot every 148 days, 27 times in eleven years. The 03:40 window recurring at 06:05 an orbit later matches the 6.10-day period to the minute, and the stores arithmetic holds to the day. Better still, the physics does dramatic work. The pinhole spot of sunlight ("It was the whole Sun") is science as image, and integration is at once the method, the error and the family's habit of waiting. Nothing is over-explained; the tiger-bush passage is exposition staged as the mother arguing with herself.

There are three soft spots:
- Eleven months to 672 AU averages about 3,500 km/s, over 1 percent of light speed. That is a torch ship the story never reckons with, and it sits oddly beside stores counted to the person-day and ships four years apart.
- Lens imaging needs long integration precisely because, as Candelaria says, "Every point on that ring has the whole planet in it." A single window resolving 8 km stripes asks for faith.
- Tiger bush is the weaker foil. The wind-driven "fir waves" of balsam-fir forests are roughly parallel, evenly spaced and advance together downwind. That is the obvious non-agent explanation on a tidally locked world with a permanent wind. The report's "die-back front" caveat gestures at it, but Graciela's "They'll believe it" then reads as more naive than the story intends.

**The two women**

The reunion opens on the best sentence in the story, "Her mother was smaller than her handwriting," and the voices hold. Graciela is clipped, says "copy" for "yes", and is soothed by a logged clock error. Candelaria deflects into tea, bunks, stores and the tone she keeps for the agency. The thread about the Chilean double surname is handled with tact, and "The agency needed an answer." / "So did I." is the dialogue at its best. The last scene turns written: "I didn't know it a week ago" narrates the mother's arc for her.

**Structure and the ending**

The story runs in three movements with alternating viewpoint, the discovery at the centre, and a clean clock: any answer lands on day 18, and the ship leaves on day 16. Graciela slipping out "before her mother was up," when we know Candelaria wakes at three, is quietly good planting. The crossed sacrifices are Gift of the Magi, but they are motivated, since the daughter learned timing from her mother, and the joint decision rescues the formula.

Still, the echoes are audible: the mugs, the lentil, "You timed it" and "She timed it", the rehearsed and unrehearsed speeches, and a finished sentence to close. Kaya's "Or neither" hands the women their answer. The cost is on the page as arithmetic, nearly three years of food against a four-year ship, but never as fear. Nobody notices that staying short of food forces Earth's hand, which is the same move they have just stopped making on each other.

**Strongest and weakest**

The three strongest passages:
- "which was the fastest she had answered her mother in a decade": the theme acted out in a four-minute calibration.
- "This lives in theirs.": first contact carried by a pronoun.
- "The stores are fourteen hundred and sixty person-days.": the mother saying yes in the only language she trusts.

The three weakest:
- "She had not known she was going to say the last thing until it was said": a stock beat that deflates the line before it.
- "an expression she could not read": stock phrasing, and Kaya's tearful laugh signals the twist.
- "it will have been true for a whole day that you could still have gone": the right idea tied in a knot of tenses.

One line edit: after "Then the arithmetic came," the next "she" briefly reads as Graciela.

**Revisions**

First, cut "Or neither" and let the stores arithmetic be how the mother reaches "both" herself. Then give the bet one beat of fear, or let one of them see that they are handing Earth a fait accompli. Second, put the fir-wave alternative in the report, and make the closing near-infrared pass the test that tells cutting from die-back.

**Verdict**

I'd buy it after a light pass on the last scene. The science is exact and does emotional work, the women are real, and a decade of patience averaging a harvest into stillness is an idea only science fiction can carry.
