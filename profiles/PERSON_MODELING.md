# Person Modeling Profile

Use this profile only after reading the current *Meaning Model* and *Life
Simulation* papers and the common modeling protocol. For a real person, obtain
consent for the material, purpose, storage, and intended audience.

## Purpose

Help a person inspect one bounded period of life as a transparent chronological
model. The result is a revisable aid to reflection, not a diagnosis or a claim
to reveal the person's true inner state.

## Three linked views

Build one shared evidence history and three viewpoint-indexed interpretations:

1. **External event history:** what was observed, reported, decided, and done,
   including changing circumstances and consequences.
2. **Candidate internal models:** several AI hypotheses about what may have
   been salient, meaningful, wanted, feared, believed, or strategically
   available.
3. **Reported self-model:** the person's own present account, including
   uncertainty and later reinterpretation.

Do not merge these views. The person's report has authority as a report; it is
not automatically a complete causal account. The AI's inference is an estimate;
it is not privileged access to the person.

## Suggested record for each wanted future

- target region or protected condition;
- holder, scope, importance, and time horizon;
- salience and believed attainability over time;
- supporting, instrumental, and conflicting wants;
- feared losses or obstructions;
- strategies believed to serve it;
- observations and reports offered as evidence;
- uncertainty and competing explanations; and
- later consequences that support or weaken the hypothesis.

Fear, concern, attachment, threat estimation, bodily arousal, and defensive
narrowing should remain separable. A person may care strongly while calm, or
feel fear because of a mistaken belief. A named emotion becomes causally
operative in the model only when it changes attention, interpretation,
strategy, decision, or action through a declared route.

## Data before functions

Start by mapping sampled values or intervals: events, reported feelings,
estimated stress, active wants, decisions, and relationship changes. Add a
transition function only after repeated evidence supports it or when the person
and agent explicitly use it as an exploratory hypothesis. Preserve the earlier
samples even if the proposed function is later rejected.

## Questions and correction

The LLM decides what to ask; the engine does not prescribe an interview.
Prefer questions that discriminate between live alternatives, such as whether
an avoided conversation protected a relationship, self-image, immediate
comfort, safety, or something else. Record that asking can itself change later
state.

The person can reject, rename, correct, restrict, export, or request deletion
of any field. Corrections should preserve provenance and the history of the
model's revision.

## Output contract

A person-facing projection should include:

- a concise accepted chronology;
- the person's own account in their words;
- candidate wants, fears, beliefs, strategies, and transitions with confidence;
- important disagreements between viewpoints;
- evidence for and against each major inference;
- unanswered questions; and
- a clear statement that the model is non-diagnostic and revisable.

Avoid personality scores or labels unless they help the person's declared
purpose and their uncertainty is visible. Never convert the portrait into an
employment, insurance, credit, clinical, or coercive decision without a
separate lawful and validated process.

The optional `life_profile_compile` MCP tool can encode the three linked views
and their shared evidence in an ordinary Rust model. It does not interview the
person, infer the nodes, claim an operative inner model, register the result,
or persist it. Those interpretation, consent, review, and explicit-registration
steps remain outside compilation.
