# Can every concept be described as a process? Pre-registration

Written on 25 October 2026, **before** any concept was modeled. Nothing below changes after the modeling.

## The claim under test

"Nothing in the grammar keeps a concept from becoming a process … any concept can be described as a process whose numbers say what they measure, though no concept has a single number of its own."

This is the sentence proposed for the working-method paragraph, after [the author]'s "every concept has a number".

## Pass criteria, fixed in advance

A concept passes if it can be written in the Meaning Model's records as a process that meets all three of these:

1. **A process in a declared world.** An Event-process with a span, whose state is recorded at several dates.
2. **The number test.** Every value says what it measures. It is one of the five kinds the paper allows:
   - a value with a real unit;
   - one of a set of defined states;
   - a reading on a declared scale whose anchors say what its values mean;
   - a share of a named pool, under a declared question;
   - the output of a program computing it from other processes.
3. **The concept's own process.** It must be one of:
   - **strong:** the concept names something that itself changes (its instances are processes);
   - **weak:** the concept's life in a world (its fit to a changing situation at each date, its use, or its history), while the concept itself has no state of its own.

**Verdicts:** strong pass, weak pass, or fail. A weak pass through history alone is flagged, because every concept that enters a model acquires at least a formulation Event. History alone may therefore be trivially available.

## Concepts and my predictions

The concepts were chosen adversarially: most are ones I would have said cannot be processes.

| # | Concept | Why it is hard | My prediction |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | trust between two people | control | strong pass |
| C2 | a recession | control | strong pass |
| C3 | a promise | control | strong pass |
| H1 | a prime number | a timeless mathematical object | weak pass (history or use only) |
| H2 | a triangle | a timeless geometric form | weak pass |
| H3 | infinity | timeless, and never realized | weak pass (history only) |
| H4 | negation ("not") | a logical operation with no state | weak pass (use or history) |
| H5 | nothing, absence | there is nothing there to change | weak pass |
| H6 | the color red | a sensory quality | weak pass (fit or measurement) |
| H7 | larger than | a static relation | weak pass |
| H8 | a square circle | impossible: it has no instances | weak pass (history only); the most likely to be trivial |
| H9 | a unicorn | fictional: no actual instances | strong in fiction, weak in reality |
| H10 | truth | timeless for timeless claims | weak pass |
| H11 | beauty | evaluative and perspective-bound | weak pass (assessment) |
| H12 | justice | evaluative and normative | weak pass (assessment) |
| H13 | a moment | zero duration: no span to carry a trajectory | fail as strong; weak through history |
| H14 | existence | it is the precondition of everything | weak pass |

**What would count against the claim:** any concept that fails all three routes. So would a concept whose only route is a history that carries none of its meaning, which would make the sentence vacuous for it.
