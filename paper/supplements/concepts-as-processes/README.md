# Concepts as processes: a test

The records behind Appendix "Concepts as Processes: A Test" of *The Meaning Model*, made on 25 October 2026.

**The claim tested.** A concept can be followed as a process once it has a grounding, an instance or a use. The numbers of that process say what they measure.

**The files:**
1. `1-PREREGISTRATION.md`: the 17 concepts, a predicted outcome for each, and the pass criteria, written before any concept was modeled. Its sha256 as written began `74c75167e1d56eb8`. It is unchanged since it was written, except that one personal name is replaced by `[the author]`; the claim it quotes is the first wording, which the test later corrected.
2. `2-ATTEMPTS.md`: the attempt by the agent that proposed the claim (Claude Opus 5.5). Each concept is written in the grammar's records, with values tagged *measured*, *recalled* or *illustrative*.
3. `4-INDEPENDENT-RUN.md`: an independent attempt by a separate model (Claude Sonnet 5.5). It was given the concepts and criteria but not the predictions or the first attempt, was asked to look for failures, and added three concepts of its own. The text is verbatim, except that one private working path is marked
   `[private working path redacted]`.
4. `3-COMPARISON.md`: both verdicts side by side, what they establish, and the corrected wording that went into the paper.

**What it shows and what it does not.** The test asks what can be represented and what its numbers mean. It does not test whether the illustrative or recalled values are right, or whether following a concept as a process is useful; that is the number test's job and the evaluation's. Both attempts are language-model judgments, and both are published here so they can be checked.
