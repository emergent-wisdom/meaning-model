**Judgment: true with a needed rewording.**

No concept among the 17 fails outright. Four pass strongly (existence trivially) and thirteen weakly. Three of the weak passes are thin (negation, square circle, truth), and the moment holds only trivially: the instant has no process and only the word's frequency moves. A rule requiring the numbers to carry the concept's meaning would turn those four into fails. The weak route is close to unconditional: any concept with two checks can be fitted to a changing situation, and any concept with a literature has a history. So "any concept can be described as a process" is true, but its content sits in a split the sentence hides. The paper already works this way for pattern cards (Patterns over time). The Concept record itself stays a Concept (line 1330); the process sits on the records around it. The one clean failure is a Concept with no grounding, instance or use (Extra 1), which line 433 anticipates.

Second clause. "No single number of its own" misleads, because a definition carries numbers: 7, pi, a 90% threshold, 180 degrees. The defensible line is that no concept has a dated state value of its own. A number in the definition is fixed by it; the numbers that vary belong to instances, situations and judgements at dates. The speed of light shows the line: its dated values belong to measurement Events (Fizeau 1849 about 3.1e8 m/s; Evenson 1972 299,792,456.2 +/- 1.1 m/s; both r), and in 1983 the number moved into the definition (exact, r) and stopped being a state.

Rewording: "Nothing in the grammar keeps a concept from being followed as a process once it has a grounding, an instance or a use. If the concept names something that changes, its instances are processes. If not, what is followed is its fit to a changing situation, its use or its history. In every case the numbers say what they measure. Those that vary sit on instances, situations and judgements at dates; a number in the concept's own definition, such as a threshold or a constant, is fixed by it and is not a state."
