# Can every concept be described as a process? My attempts

These attempts were written after the pre-registration (`1-PREREGISTRATION.md`, sha256 `74c75167…`), and its criteria and predictions are applied unchanged. Each concept is written in the Meaning Model's records:

- the world;
- the Things;
- the Event-process and its span;
- the value kind;
- dated values, each tagged *measured*, *recalled* (from memory, unchecked) or *illustrative* (invented to show the form);
- the route;
- the verdict.

## The controls

**C1. Trust between two people.**
- **World:** the first *Book of Conditions*, 1843–1852.
- **Records:**
  - **Things:** Lovelace and Babbage.
  - **Event-process:** Lovelace's trust in Babbage, an inner Event-process beneath her lifecycle and inner-process root, linked *about* Babbage.
  - **Value:** a share of a named pool. Question: of the Engine decisions on which she relied on another person's judgment that year, what share did she leave to Babbage, to the others (Neale, Halden, or her own checking), and what remains?
- **Values** (illustrative):

  | Year | Babbage | Others | Remainder |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1843 | .70 | .20 | .10 |
  | 1847 | .50 | .40 | .10 |
  | 1851 | .30 | .60 | .10 |

- **Route:** strong. Trust itself changes.
- **Verdict:** strong pass.

**C2. A recession.**
- **World:** the actual United Kingdom, 2007–2010.
- **Records:**
  - **Thing:** the UK economy, a collective Thing.
  - **Event-process:** the 2008–09 recession, inside the economy's lifecycle.
  - **Values:** quarterly real GDP (unit: pounds at constant prices), plus a defined state from {expansion, recession, recovery}.
- **Values** (recalled):
  - GDP fell about 6% from its peak in early 2008 to its trough in mid-2009.
  - The state is expansion in 2008 Q1, recession from 2008 Q2, and recovery from late 2009.
- **Route:** strong.
- **Verdict:** strong pass.

**C3. A promise.**
- **World:** illustrative. A builder promises to finish a house by 1 June.
- **Records:**
  - **Things:** the builder, the client and the house.
  - **Event-process:** the promise, a joint Event binding promisor and promisee, from its making to its keeping or breaking.
  - **Values:** a defined state from {made, on track, at risk, broken, kept}, and the share of the promised work done (pool: the promised tasks).
- **Values** (illustrative): week 1, made, .05; week 6, at risk, .40; week 10, kept, 1.00.
- **Route:** strong.
- **Verdict:** strong pass.

## The hard cases

**H1. A prime number.**
- **The strong route fails.** Seven was prime before anyone lived and will be prime after; nothing about primeness changes.
- **History.**
  - **World:** actual mathematics, 1951–2024.
  - **Event-process:** the record for the largest known prime.
  - **Value:** its decimal digits (unit: digits).
  - **Values** (recalled): 687 in 1952, 420,921 in 1996, 24,862,048 in 2018, 41,024,320 in 2024.
- **Use.**
  - **World:** illustrative, one pupil over an autumn term.
  - **Value:** the share of the numbers 1–100 the pupil classifies correctly as prime or composite (pool: the hundred numbers).
  - **Values** (illustrative): .55, .72, .90, week by week.
- **Route:** weak, through use and history. Both carry real content: the reach of the concept and a learner's grasp of it.
- **Verdict:** weak pass.

**H2. A triangle.**
- **The strong route fails.** The form does not change.
- **Fit.**
  - **World:** illustrative, a tent on a windy night.
  - **Records:**
    - **Thing:** the tent.
    - **Event-process:** its shape through the storm.
    - **Realization:** a *describe* Realization to *triangle*, with a fit Cut on an assessment Event every half hour. The comparison budget is a frozen set of checks: three straight edges within tolerance, angles summing to 180° within 5°, and closure.
  - **Values** (illustrative), as the matches share: .95 at 22:00, .60 at 23:30 (one side bowed), .20 at 01:00 (collapsed).
- **Route:** weak, through fit. The concept's hold on a changing thing is followed, as in "Patterns over time".
- **Verdict:** weak pass, with a trajectory that says something real about the tent.

**H3. Infinity.**
- **The strong route fails.** Infinity has no instances in time.
- **History.**
  - **World:** actual mathematics, 1800–1930.
  - **Thing:** the mathematical community.
  - **Event-process:** the standing of completed (actual) infinity.
  - **Value:** a defined state from {rejected (potential infinity only), contested, accepted}.
  - **Values** (recalled): 1830, rejected (Gauss's 1831 protest against the completed infinite); 1880, contested (Cantor's work, Kronecker's opposition); 1930, accepted in mainstream mathematics (set theory axiomatized, Hilbert's defence).
- **Route:** weak, through history. This history changes what the concept is taken to be, which the grammar records as new grounding versions linked by supersession. It is the history of a definition, not of a use count.
- **Verdict:** weak pass, and far from trivial.

**H4. Negation ("not").**
- **The strong route fails.** A logical operation has no state.
- **Use.**
  - **World:** illustrative, one child's speech between 18 and 36 months.
  - **Value:** the share of the child's negative utterances (pool) that place "not" inside the sentence rather than a sentence-initial "no".
  - **Values** (illustrative): 18 months, .00; 24 months, .30; 30 months, .80.
- **History.** Negation in logic: Frege's 1879 notation, then the split in 1930 into classical and intuitionistic negation. The grammar records the split as linked successor Concepts.
- **Route:** weak, through use and history.
- **Verdict:** weak pass.

**H5. Nothing, absence.**
- **Absence of something is a state of a process.**
  - **World:** illustrative, a river in a drought summer.
  - **Event-process:** the river's flow at a gauge (unit: m³/s).
  - **Values** (illustrative): June 2.1, July 0.4, August 0.0. The absence of water is the recorded state.
- **"Nothing" as such: history.**
  - **World:** actual history, 600–1500.
  - **Event-process:** the spread of a zero numeral.
  - **Value:** a defined state per region from {absent, known, standard}.
- **Route:** strong for "absence of X"; weak, through history, for "nothing" in the abstract.
- **Verdict:** strong pass (absence of X), weak pass ("nothing").

**H6. The color red.**
- **Fit or measurement.**
  - **World:** illustrative, a tomato ripening.
  - **Event-process:** the tomato's surface color.
  - **Value:** the share of its surface classified red under a declared colorimetric threshold, from a fixed photo protocol (pool: surface pixels). A real unit, dominant wavelength in nm, would also serve.
  - **Values** (illustrative): day 1, .05; day 5, .40; day 9, .95.
- **History.** Color terms across languages (recalled: in Berlin and Kay's ordering, red is the first color term after dark and light).
- **Route:** weak, through fit and measurement. The concept's degree in a changing thing is followed. The line between this and "strong" is blurry for qualities: reddening is a process, but red is not.
- **Verdict:** weak pass, with real units.

**H7. Larger than.**
- **World:** illustrative, two children, A and B.
- **Records:**
  - **Things:** A and B.
  - **Processes:** their heights (unit: cm).
  - **The relation:** "A is taller than B" is a process computed from the two heights. This is the fifth kind of value: a state from {larger, equal, smaller}, or the difference in cm.
- **Values** (illustrative): age 8, 128 vs 131 cm, smaller; age 12, 150 vs 149, larger; age 15, 170 vs 162, larger.
- **Route:** strong. An instance of the relation holds or fails over time, just as trust does. The grammar already says a relation can have its own history.
- **Verdict:** strong pass. My prediction (weak) was wrong.

**H8. A square circle.**
- **The strong route fails.** No instance can exist. Every fit is "does not match", a constant and degenerate series.
- **History.** The concept's career in philosophy: Meinong's impossible objects (1904), Russell's theory of descriptions (1905). The value is the concept's standing, a defined state from {an object of reference, an example of a non-referring description}.
  - Note: the related problem of squaring the circle is a different concept. It has a richer history: its standing changed from open to proven impossible in 1882 (Lindemann).
- **Route:** weak, through history only.
- **Verdict:** weak pass, flagged as nearly trivial. The process exists, but it says almost nothing about the concept itself, whose meaning is a fixed contradiction. This is the closest any concept came to a counterexample.

**H9. A unicorn.**
- **In fiction.**
  - **World:** illustrative, a fantasy novel.
  - **Thing:** the unicorn.
  - **Event-process:** its life, with its strength on an anchored scale and its trust in the heroine as a share. These are fictional canon.
- **In the actual world.** There are no instances, only the history of belief: the share of European natural-history books (pool: those in a fixed bibliography, per half-century) that list the unicorn as a real animal. Illustrative values: high in 1550, low by 1750.
- **Route:** strong in fiction; weak, through history, in reality.
- **Verdict:** strong pass in fiction, weak pass in reality.

**H10. Truth.**
- **For a claim about a changing world, truth changes.**
  - **World:** actual. The claim is "the Berlin Wall stands".
  - **Value:** a defined state from {true, false}.
  - **Values:** true until 9 November 1989, false after.
- **Timeless claims** do not change their truth; only assessment of them changes. This is the paper's epistemic assessment Cut over support, rejection and unresolved. That is belief about truth, a weak route.
- **Route:** strong for temporal claims; weak, through assessment and history, for timeless ones.
- **Verdict:** strong pass for temporal claims, weak pass otherwise.

**H11. Beauty.**
- **World:** illustrative, a public garden through a year.
- **Value:** the share of visitors surveyed each month (pool) who call it beautiful. This is attributed and perspective-bound. An evaluator's anchored scale would also serve, from 1, "neglected", to 5, "at its height".
- **Values** (illustrative): April .60, June .85, November .30.
- **Route:** weak, through assessment.
- **Verdict:** weak pass. It is the same form as the paper's fit Cuts for kindness and situated intelligence.

**H12. Justice.**
- **World:** illustrative, a court system across decades.
- **Values:** the share of sampled rulings each year that satisfy a frozen justice grounding's checks (pool: the sampled rulings). This is an assessment held by an evaluator. Public perception could be added as the share of people surveyed who say the courts are fair.
- **Route:** weak, through assessment.
- **Verdict:** weak pass.

**H13. A moment.**
- **The strong route fails.** A moment has zero duration, so there is no span inside it to carry a trajectory. In the grammar a moment is an Event with a tagged instant: a point inside processes, not a process.
- **History.**
  - **World:** actual.
  - **Event-process:** the shortest interval people could measure.
  - **Unit:** seconds.
  - **Values** (recalled, orders of magnitude): femtoseconds by the 1990s, attoseconds from 2001.
- **Route:** weak, through history. It carries real meaning: how finely an instant can be resolved.
- **Verdict:** weak pass. The strong-route failure was predicted, and it held.

**H14. Existence.**
- **World:** actual. The Things are the passenger pigeon as a species and the Berlin Wall.
- **Records:** in this grammar every Thing has exactly one lifecycle Event, so existence is modeled as a process.
  - **Values:** a defined state from {not yet, exists, no longer}, or a count of living individuals.
  - **Values** (recalled): the pigeons numbered in the billions in the early 1800s, and the last one died in 1914. The Wall exists from 1961 and is breached in 1989.
- **Route:** strong.
- **Verdict:** strong pass. My prediction (weak) was wrong.

**No concept failed.** Four predictions were too pessimistic: absence of something, larger than, truth of a temporal claim, and existence. In each, the grammar already treats the concept as a process: a zero state, a computed relation, a claim's truth value, a lifecycle. The square circle is the one case where the process says almost nothing about the concept itself.
