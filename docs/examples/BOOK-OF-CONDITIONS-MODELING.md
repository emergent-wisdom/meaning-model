# Modeling with The Book of Conditions

The Book develops a counterfactual world through
coarse history, whole lives, selected scenes, and explicit revisions. Its method
is useful beyond this story: explore what a process or choice opens onto,
follow its connections, and let discoveries change the model and the writing.
Its categories are replaceable choices, not a required ontology for people.
The characters' invented interiors are fiction, including those of historical
people.

The current example uses the revised native model and manuscript in
`examples/book-of-conditions/`, with a publication manifest identifying the
edition. The paper separately reports the first construction's historical
measurements; those earlier results are not new evaluations of this revision.

## Keep a life wider than the scene's emotional composition

The Book's **IS** view uses nine concurrent life processes: body, kin,
partnership, work, place, means, knowledge, standing, and meaning. They coexist
and may overlap. They are not children of one normalized Cut and do not sum
to one. Illness can change bodily capacity while work, relationships, and
standing change on different timescales. Registered people, institutions,
documents, and tools retain their identities through the relevant Events.

The bundled `person_scaffold` offers these process addresses as an optional
starting point. It does not supply a life history or semantic scores. Use other
processes, or fewer of them, when they explain the application better.

At a selected scene, keep these questions distinct:

| Account | What it contributes |
|---|---|
| Knowledge and belief | What this actor has encountered, learned, inferred, or misunderstood by this time. The actor need not know everything the author knows. |
| **WHAT**: wants and concerns | Which endpoints the person seeks or protects, and how represented motivational attention is divided. |
| Fulfillment outlook | Whether wanted endpoints seem secured, threatened, or unresolved. A conditional threat Cut may open which wants seem at risk. |
| **HOW**: problem solving | How represented attention is divided among framing and deciding, with further conditional distinctions when useful. |
| **FEELS**: appraisal | The scene's emotional composition, grounded in the actor's wants, beliefs, and situation. Threat can produce anger, sadness, bargaining, or action as well as fear. |

Each Cut declares its Event parent, question, divided unit, alternatives,
conditioning, and remainder. Babbage's trial outlook allocates `.76` to assured
fulfillment, `.18` to threatened fulfillment, and `.06` to unresolved outlook.
These are authored shares of one outlook unit, not success probabilities or
absolute emotional intensities. A FEELS Cut answers a separate question.
Do not manufacture a common score by averaging unrelated Cuts.

The accepted Book used concern-level scene WHAT Cuts and qualitative want
Events. Deeper numerical AtomicWant trees appeared in preparation and template
designs; they were not all exercised in the accepted scenes. A template's
available depth is not evidence that a particular character already has it.

## Let material and institutional constraints explain consequences

The calculation office estimates 40 independent checker-hours per final job
packet and protects 80 checker-hours per quarter. Four final packets therefore
require 160 hours: an 80-hour shortfall. These are ordinary work quantities,
not normalized psychological shares. Earlier preparation uses provisional
figures; final signed sources arrive in the delivery quarter, so that work
does not remove the independent comparison required for the final packets.

Keep the intermediate Events and authority visible. Babbage urges expansion;
Halden holds contracting authority and accepts four jobs. Lovelace identifies
the checking and succession risk. Funding another independent checker is
deferred. Later, a transcription error and Halden's choice to deliver before
the duplicate check let a wrong table leave the office. Lovelace's death
removes her active participation; it neither inserts the error nor forces
that delivery decision.

This opens an emotional arc through its world. Ask whether confidence is based
on the same successful trial, whether recognition changes what each founder
wants, and who can commit the office's money or labor. Add explicit model
relations and attributed Understanding Nodes for those explanations. A causal
link records the account being proposed; it does not execute a mechanism or
prove the explanation merely by existing.

## Make voice depend on the person and the encounter

The Book's rendering revisions distinguish Babbage's tendency to classify a
threat as a question about mechanisms, Lovelace's movement from uncertainty
toward explicit conditions, and Halden's movement from bounded accounting
through promotion to self-audit. Their shared vocabulary can remain shared;
different attention, stakes, and histories make it serve different purposes.
A change in voice may emerge through interruption, handling an object,
withholding a claim, or choosing a professional term.

Explore the relevant knowledge, wants, outlook, relationships, and constraints
before assigning another voice adjective. Give the author or narrator a
separate account of attention and disclosure. The author may know an eventual
failure while the speaker still expects success. Change the world model when
a revision introduces a new fact; record a rendering decision when it changes
only how existing facts reach the reader.

## Apply the method through the MCP

Import the example's portable model with `life_construction_import`. For
continuing work, use `life_construction_replay` and `life_model_outline`
before changing it, then inspect the records needed for the current question.
For new work, the bundled
[minimal model and graph example](MINIMAL-MODEL-AND-GRAPH.md) supplies valid
payloads; this example supplies modeling judgments rather than a compulsory
cast or category list.

Choose an opening from curiosity as well as from an explanatory gap. A process
may suggest unfamiliar sub-processes, relationships, concepts, voices or worlds;
play, surprise or a new way of writing can reveal a question no checklist names.
Follow those discoveries recursively: open a promising connection, learn what
it reveals, revise the account, and revisit the connected regions. A useful
model can keep growing without first being found defective.

Store questions, alternatives and reasons with `life_understanding_record`,
linked to the actual records. Provisional drafts can help discover what to
model next; save them as drafts rather than silently adopting their new facts.
Before accepting the resulting scene, reconcile its commitments and complete
the applicable reviews. This exploration remains within the existing creative
delegation, without a separate approval for every new question.

Keep a region coarse when it is **sufficient here**, and say what might reopen
it. That is a local stopping judgment, not a prohibition on later curiosity or
a demand to exhaust every branch. For example, a customer's remaining biography
can stay unresolved while the signed-source arrival and delivery obligation
explain the bottleneck. If the accepted account instead asserts that four
packets received independent checking, it needs to account for that capacity.
Unknowns and competing interpretations may remain part of the intended story.

Preserve the declared parent commitments when opening compatible children;
revise the parent openly if the finer account contradicts it. Selected scenes
do not automatically form a complete duration-weighted partition of a life,
and sparse appraisals do not establish a continuous psychological curve.
Follow the storytelling add-on's current depth and scene preparation, review,
and commit workflow. Open the result with `life_model_viewer_open` to inspect
the records, their relations, and the prose together. A successful structural
check does not establish explanatory depth or literary quality.

For the full account and its limits, read the Book-specific profile and worked
construction sections of `life-sim://theory/meaning-model`. For category
revision, read `life-sim://example/application-categories`.
