# The number test

A Meaning Model states understanding as numbers that change over time. A category is worth carving only if two
things hold:
- a number can be put on it at one date after another;
- a reader can tell what each of those numbers means.

This document is that test, with worked examples for [the process breakdown](PROCESS-BREAKDOWN.md). Each example
uses illustrative numbers to show whether its form makes sense. The verdict concerns the form and meaning of a
measure, not the factual accuracy of its example values.

Run the test on your own categories before you record their values. It decides whether a category is worth guessing
at all, and whether the structure you build makes sense to someone else reading it.

## The test

Ask six questions of every category, and write the answers down.

1. **What form carries it?** Prefer the forms in this order:
   - **a value with a real unit,** such as hours, money, people, doses, letters a month or kilometres;
   - **one of a set of defined states,** such as single, married, separated or widowed;
   - **a reading on a scale whose anchors say what the values mean;**
   - **a share under a Cut,** when categories compete for one pool;
   - **the output of a program,** when a law computes it from other values.

   A real unit beats a scale whenever one exists. Record drops of laudanum a day, not "opiate use from 0 to 10".
2. **What does each value mean?**
   - **A unit** says it.
   - **States:** each one is defined, and no two can be true at once.
   - **A scale:** every section of it says what it means. Anchor a 0–10 scale at least every two points: 0, 2, 4, 6,
     8 and 10. Make each anchor something a reader could observe. If a reader cannot tell a 6 from a 7, do not record
     6.5; record only the steps the anchors support.
   - **A Cut:** name the pool the shares divide, in something that can be counted or pictured, such as waking hours,
     decisions, acts, money, words, people or land. Every category must draw on that same pool. "One unit of her
     motivational attention" names no pool, so a share of 0.3 of it means nothing a reader can check.
3. **What can it be compared against?** It can be compared against itself over time, against other people, against
   the person's other states, or against the truth. A number that can be compared with nothing is not worth recording.
4. **Can it be guessed?** Say from what evidence, and with what band. Tag the value `source`, `inferred`, `invented`
   or `sketch`. An honest guess with a wide band passes. A number that no evidence could move does not.
5. **What moves it?** Name the Events that change it. A value that nothing could move is a constant, not a process.
6. **The verdict:** yes; yes after a stated revision; or no. Give the reason.

Not every node in a tree is a number. A Thing's identity is a criterion written in words. A container, such as a
person's "body", holds processes and carries no value of its own. The test applies to the processes inside it.

### Composition is not intensity

A Cut divides one pool, so it shows composition: of everything in the pool, how much falls to each category. It cannot
show how much there is.

Consider a fictional character's feelings recorded only as a Cut:
- **During an illness.** Joy 0.10, sadness 0.25, fear 0.15 and anger 0.15.
- **An ordinary period.** Joy 0.20, sadness 0.20.
- **After a success.** Joy 0.20, the same as in the ordinary period.

Nothing in those numbers says how strongly she felt any of them. An optional intensity scale beside the Cut can
show that distinction. Omitting it leaves intensity unknown; it does not make the composition reading invalid.

The same holds wherever things can all rise together. Someone in a crisis can want safety, their children and
their work more at once, and can love two people more at once. When their strength is modeled, each want and each
love gets its own measure; the Cut tells only how they divide something they compete for, such as the hours or the
choices of a period. Feeling intensities, want strengths and causal differences are optional. The test checks their
meaning when recorded; they are not additional completion requirements.

### Anchoring a scale

Every 0–10 scale carries its own anchors in its definition, at least every two points. [The process
breakdown](PROCESS-BREAKDOWN.md) holds the anchors for every scale this test recommends, from the intensity of a
feeling to the condition of a machine's part, in one table under "The number test". The intensity of any feeling,
for example:

| Value | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | not present |
| 2 | noticed, but changes nothing they do |
| 4 | colours some choices; others could notice it |
| 6 | shapes most decisions; hard to set aside |
| 8 | dominates the day; sleep or work suffer |
| 10 | overwhelming; nothing else can be thought |

Where a recognised instrument exists, its scale is already anchored and can be used or imitated:
- pain from 0 to 10 (the numeric rating scale), whose anchors the breakdown's table uses;
- anxiety on the GAD-7, from 0 to 21;
- low mood on the PHQ-9, from 0 to 27;
- self-esteem on the Rosenberg scale;
- loneliness on the UCLA scale.

## Common modeling errors

These examples show what the test asks the modeler to change.

| Issue | Example | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Composition is mistaken for intensity.** | Joy has the same share in an ordinary period and after a success. | **Keep the distinction.** An optional intensity scale can show how strongly joy is felt; the Cut alone cannot. |
| **A Cut names no pool.** | "One unit of motivational attention", with execution 0.40 and understanding 0.20: 0.40 of what? | **Revise.** Name the pool: waking hours, choices, acts, money, words or people. |
| **An unweighted cause is treated as a ranked one.** | Two Events are linked to an outcome, but neither has a counterfactual reading. | **Keep the links.** They name causes but do not rank them. Where a difference can be judged, record it with `life_cause_record`. Weights are optional. |
| **The outlook Cut has two meanings.** | Assured 0.30, threatened 0.70 could mean a felt chance or a share of wants. | **Revise.** Here outlook is the share of wants, weighted as in the wants Cut, seen as threatened. A felt chance for a particular want is a separate value. |
| **A scale leaves its steps unexplained.** | Health moves 4.3, 4.8, 5.5, but only 0, 5 and 10 have anchors. | **Revise.** Anchor at least every two points and record only the precision those anchors support. |
| **A scale replaces an available unit.** | Substance use is anchored by doses but recorded from 0 to 10. | **Revise.** Use doses per day. |
| **One scale holds two things.** | "Closeness and trust" falls from 7 to 2.5, although someone can stay close while concealing a debt. | **Revise.** Two things that can move apart are two processes. |
| **A container is expected to have a value.** | `body` holds health, sleep and illnesses. | **Keep the container.** The processes inside it carry the values. |
| **A label claims more than its measure.** | "Belonging" is people per household. | **Revise the label.** Household size does not measure belonging. |
| **A sound form is mistaken for a verified fact.** | A population count has a clear unit but lacks a cited source. | **Check the value.** Passing the number test does not verify a factual estimate. |

## Every example, tested

All values below are illustrative sketches for testing a measure's meaning, not verified observations or claims
about the world. Examples about a person's life concern a fictional character, not a historical biography. The
verdicts are:
- **Yes;**
- **Revise:** yes after the change stated;
- **No:** drop the category, or carry it as text or as states.
